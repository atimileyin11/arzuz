What the badges mean

Nothing appears above without a badge, and the badge is the honest answer to "how far did you check?". An Inspected badge names the parts — the checks below the badge are what the officer actually recorded, with the date, and they stop counting after six months:

  • Inspected — someone from Arzuz stood in the room, and the listing says which of these they checked: water pressure at the tap, the power supply and meter, whether the photos match the room, the price the owner charges, the way in (and whether the road floods), and the gate and security. Each check carries the officer's own sentence about what they saw, and the date. The record is good for six months; after that the badge reads Re-check due rather than pretending the visit is recent.
  • Community verified — a tenant living in the building told us what they pay and what actually works. The rent is real; nobody from Arzuz has been inside yet, and the badge says so.
  • Unverified — the owner listed it and we have not checked it. Treat the price as the asking price, not the confirmed one.

"Inspected" is a claim with parts, so we will not print it for a room whose record has run out or whose visit predates this. A room that says Re-check due is one we owe another visit — ask us and we will go.

How to read a listing

Prices are for one session, not per month, and they are what the owner says they are charging — Arzuz adds no agent fee on top. The walking time is measured from the pin the owner dropped at the gate rather than estimated, so two rooms on the same street can honestly show different numbers.

Contact goes through the Arzuz team. The owner's number is never published and neither is yours. Arzuz takes no rent and holds no deposit — you pay the owner directly, and only after you have seen the room. Renting costs you nothing on Arzuz; the only thing it sells is an optional Care Pass for repairs.
