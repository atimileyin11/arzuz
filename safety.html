This page is what we tell every student before they pay any rent: how far our checks actually go, what to look at in a compound, the patterns that turn into a scam, and who to talk to when something goes wrong.

How far our checks go — and what we cannot promise

Every listing carries one of three badges. It is the honest answer to "how far did this check go?", and it is worth reading before you treat a price as real:

  • Inspected — someone from Arzuz stood in the room. The rent, the utilities and the walking distance are confirmed on site.
  • Community verified — a tenant living in the building told us what they pay and what actually works. The rent is real; nobody from Arzuz has been inside yet.
  • Unverified — the owner listed it and we have not checked it. Treat the price as an asking price, not a confirmed one.

What we cannot promise. Arzuz does not own these compounds and does not collect your rent. You pay the owner directly, which is also why there is no agent fee to anyone. A badge tells you how much checking was done — it is not a guarantee about a landlord, a neighbour or a road. So the checks below matter on an inspected room as much as on an unverified one.

Ask for the total. "What do I pay on the day I collect the key, all in?" If that number is not the rent you were quoted, the bigger number is the price of the room. There is a full breakdown of what gets added on in what rent actually costs in Malete.

What to check in a compound before you pay

Walk in with the three badges forgotten and these six questions instead. A landlord who answers them plainly is worth more than a compound that photographs well.

  • Water. Where does it come from, and when does it come? A borehole that runs when the grid is off is worth money. Turn the tap on while you are standing there.
  • Power. Is there a NEPA connection, a generator, solar, or nothing? Who buys the fuel, and who decides how long a generator runs? "Generator backup" means very little if it runs for two hours at night.
  • The toilet and the bathroom. How many people share it? Do not accept a description of it — count the doors and look at the ceiling for damp.
  • The gate, and who holds the key. Is it locked at night, and who is on the compound when nobody is watching? If you keep a laptop in the room, this is the question that matters most.
  • The agreement. Who actually signs it — the owner, or someone's brother? How long is it for, and what is written on it? Ask for a copy.
  • Work you can see. A compound that looks lived in and repaired is the strongest signal there is. Ask who fixes a blown bulb, and how they are reached.

The patterns that turn into a scam

No list of scams is complete, but the ones students run into around campus share a shape: you are asked to pay, or to hurry, before you have seen the thing you are paying for. Any of these is a reason to stop and message us:

  • A fee to inspect. There is no such thing. Nobody is paid to show you a room, and Arzuz takes nothing.
  • A "holding fee" to keep a room for you. Money for a room you have not seen, from a person who will not put it in writing, is money you may not get back.
  • Pressure on the day. "Another student is coming at 4pm" is a sentence that exists to stop you checking. A room that is right will still be right tomorrow.
  • A price that changes after you agree. The rent is quoted, then a levy, a caution, or a paperwork fee appears. Get the total, in writing, before money moves.
  • Paying an agent who says they are the owner. Ask who owns the property, and ask to see something that says so. If the name on the agreement is a stranger, that is your answer.
  • A room that is already taken. If the one you asked about is suddenly gone but another — dearer — room is available, that is a sales move, not bad luck.

If any of this happens, you lose nothing by walking away. Tell us what you were asked and who asked, and we will look into the listing — a property is removed and its owner barred if it happens on Arzuz.

Viewing a room in person

View in daylight, and take someone with you. A friend, a sibling, a parent. If you have to go alone, tell us before you go and we will check in with you afterwards.

Tell someone where you are. When you tell us you are viewing a property, we can message one person you choose with the address and the time, and let them know when you confirm you are back.

If anything feels wrong, leave. You do not owe anyone an explanation, and you do not owe anyone your time because they showed you a room.

The hour you walk matters as much as the room. Walk the route you will actually use, at the time of day you will actually use it, and ask about that road in the rainy season — some routes stop being walkable, which turns one journey into another. More on that in what "near campus" is worth.

If you are under 18

We will ask for a parent or guardian's number, and we will send them everything we send you. We will not arrange a viewing for you on your own.

After you move in

Everyday problems are a different question from the ones above, and they are what Arzuz Care exists for: what is covered, and what it costs is stated on the page, including the things that are not covered.

If you leave a review, we never tell a landlord who wrote it. On a small compound we will offer to hold your review until another resident has also reviewed, so you cannot be identified by it. If a landlord ever penalises you for a review, tell us — the property is removed and the owner is barred.
