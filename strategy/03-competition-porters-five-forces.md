# 03 · Competition & industry — Porter's Five Forces

**What it answers:** who we're really up against, and where the power sits in the Nigerian
student-housing market. Five Forces decides *where* a small player can actually win — not
whether the market is "competitive".

---

## Competitive map

| Competitor | What they do | Pricing | Core features | Channels | Student sentiment | Arzuz's counter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agents (the default)** | Brokers rooms for a cut; controls information | 10% commission + fake "inspection fees" | They come to you; zero effort | Campus presence, WhatsApp | Resented but unavoidable — "no option" | Zero agent fees; prices published, not negotiated per buyer |
| **WhatsApp / Facebook hostel groups** | Free listings, chaos | Free | Photos, prices, chat | The platforms themselves | Distrustful — scam-heavy, no verification | Same channel habits, but every listing verified before it shows |
| **Landlord self-marketing** | Signboards, "TO LET" boards, word of mouth | Full rent | Nothing digital | Physical | Neutral; slow discovery | Give landlords a free verified listing page ([owner-list.html](../owner-list.html)) |
| **National property portals** (e.g. PropertyPro, Nairaland-type classifieds) | Listings for everything, everywhere | Freemium for landlords | Search filters, photos | SEO, ads | Low trust for student-specific needs (stale listings, fake posts) | Student-only, session-priced, walking-time-first, verified-only inventory |

**The gap in the market:** nobody publishes *real, verified prices* for student rooms.
Agents profit from opacity; groups have no verification; portals don't specialise. That gap
is Arzuz's entire wedge — protect it by never rounding a number up.

## Five Forces

| Force | Intensity | Why | Arzuz's play |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Threat of new entrants** | **Moderate→High** | A WhatsApp group + a form is a "competitor". But the *dataset* — verified prices, per-building history — is the barrier, and it compounds | Move fast on the Malete dataset; it cannot be copied retroactively |
| **Buyer power (students)** | **High** | Low switching cost, price-sensitive, seasonal urgency | Be free and obviously fair to students; monetise the other side + services |
| **Supplier power (landlords)** | **Low→Moderate** | Fragmented, many small landlords; but each landlord owns scarce rooms in a shortage | Verified listing = free demand; Care = paid upsell; keep their cost at zero until rooms fill |
| **Substitutes** | **High** | On-campus hostels (if allocated), staying home/commuting, agent brokers | Position against the *process*, not just rooms: "verified before you pay" beats on-campus queues and agent lotteries |
| **Existing rivalry** | **Low** | No specialised, verified, student-first incumbent in the market today | Win Malete completely before any second city; depth beats spread |

**Strategic read:** the dangerous forces are *new entrants* and *substitutes*, not rivals.
Both are beaten the same way: be the only source of verified price truth for this market,
and make the verification dataset grow every session.
